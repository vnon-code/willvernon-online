- Brief (p18): imagine writing doesn't exist yet in your culture, or has been forgotten; invent it, in six set stages:
  12 objects from a museum visit → objects in line or tone → 9 refined to pictograms → pictograms to glyphs → Glyphs app → animation.
- Pitt Rivers Museum visit, contact sheet (p19). Tone drawings (p20–21), line pictograms (p22–23), grid (p24–25), glyphs (p26–27).
- Object → glyph (p25), glyph shapes (p27): Happy mask → Haa'sk, Crazy mask → Cra'sk, Spear head → Spheed, Sad mask → Saa'sk,
  Grinning mask → Griin, Tribal pattern → T'per, Decorated shield → Corash, Scared mask → Sree'sk, Iron weight → Ir'wei.
  NB the old site's captions under glyph3–9 pair the names with the wrong glyphs.
- Glyphs app (p28): glyph 1 replaced Q, then along the row to O.
- Influences (p29–30): Jean-Pierre Ugarte (Atlantic Wall landscapes); AI as a tool, not a competitor.
- ControlNet (p32): glyph images as guides for Stable Diffusion. First tests (p33): glyphs became structures, but the prompt
  also built the background; each material needed a new prompt. Seeds and prompts (p34), 7 listed; the kept one is the last
  (seed 665821143, "remnants of a monolithic structure from a long lost civilization, realistic textures and lighting"),
  inferred from the page layout. Final nine (p35).
