## The project
- GDES5014 (folder "1st Year"), Visual Systems; due about 13 March 2024 (A3 PDF 13 Mar, animation 12 Mar 2024). The book's first half is the module's warm-up tasks; the "Extended Project" (pp.22-38) is this project.
- The brief, as the book quotes it: develop a design system for an exhibition centred on a chosen creative practitioner, with a method of promotion in a space, informative ephemera, a short animation, an example of way-finding, and an indication of content.
- Will chose Marimekko: a theoretical retrospective at the Saatchi Gallery (poster dates "Opens 05.01.2024", animation "05/01 - 10/03/2024").
- Solo project. The "Series and systems" poster task earlier in the book was done in pairs, but that is a different task (Mid-air thief audio posters).

## The idea
- Marimekko (Finland, 1951, Armi Ratia): bold prints, anti-elitist, self-expression. Book quotes Ratia: "not about trending fashion... timeless and lasting products".
- Four designers, picked for popularity and for how different their patterns are: Antti Kekki, Maija Isola, Anikka (Annika) Rimala, Fujiwo Ishimoto. 16 motifs redrawn (Rappu, Patikka, Lauha, Heinikko / Kalasaaski, Leylah, Koppelo, Staccato / Aitu, Laine, Petrooli, Pikkusuomo / KevatTalkoot, Kuiskaus, Taivas, Vihma).
- The system comes in fours: every asset has 4 variants, one per artist, each with its own pattern and colour pair. The colour code is also the wayfinding: each exhibition space is told apart by colour.
- One repeatable motif per artist is built into the logotype itself (strips of pattern above and below "marimekko"), so each artist has an identity everywhere the logo appears.

## Process beats
1. Warm-up tasks (pp.3-17): shape, texture, colour vocabulary (excitement, anger, calm, anxiety); audio-visualised posters in pairs; grid systems; shape grammars; type permutations of the word "marimekko" (distortion, slicing, blend tool).
2. Field trip (pp.18-21): Design Museum, Sound and music exhibition, Natural Science Museum. Notes on a skate exhibition using two yellow lines like a road to guide viewers, and bold text and pictograms for wayfinding.
3. Initial ideas (p.24): colours, shapes (two-tone tessellating patterns), a typewriter-rooted logotype.
4. Colour analysis (p.25) and typography (p.26): compared American Typewriter ITC, Computer Modern, Incognitype; settled on American Typewriter ITC for its boldness and resemblance to the Olivetti-typewriter logotype.
5. Artists (p.27): traced and redrew the patterns, harder than expected because sources were low resolution.
6. Exhibition identity (pp.28-29): four first logotype ideas combining features from all four artists; chose the one using a repeatable pattern from each.
7. Visual system (p.30): patterns, colours, logotype in sets of four.
8. Applications (pp.31-37): first four posters, redone at A3 with more patterns and clearer information; colour-coded floor tape and a map with arrows; four tickets (primary and secondary colours swapped to suit the logo); leaflets; billboards; exterior mock-ups (mural, flags, concrete wall).
9. Animation (p.38): a looping ad (bus-stop billboard idea). Pattern strips animated up and down the right edge, artist names centred with the source text animated, masked line wipes, "easy ease" for human-feeling movement, logotype and dates at the end so it loops perfectly; exported with Media Encoder.

## Problems met
- Patterns had to be traced or redrawn to reach usable resolution (A3 PDF, p.1).
- Posters first made small, then redesigned at A3 because the info was hard to read (p.32).
- Making all four logo ideas respect all four artists (p.28).
